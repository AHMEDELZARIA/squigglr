\section*{Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\paragraph{Purpose and intended readers.}
This Software Requirements Specification (SRS) defines the externally
observable behaviour, constraints and quality requirements of \progname.
It establishes a shared basis for the development team, the supervisor,
reviewers and future maintainers to understand, design, implement and
verify the product. It specifies \emph{what} Squigglr must provide rather
than prescribing an implementation, except where a stakeholder has imposed
a constraint. The project serves children practising drawing on a
stylus-enabled touchscreen and the supporting adults who configure
activities and review practice records (\srcref{001}, \srcref{004}).
The scope, stakeholders, goals and constraints are established in
Sections~\ref{sec:purpose}--\ref{sec:constraints}; requirements are refined
in Section~\ref{sec:functional} and the subsequent quality sections.

\paragraph{Template and adaptations.}
This document follows the Volere Requirements Specification Template by
Suzanne Robertson and James Robertson~\cite{robertson2012volere}; its
LaTeX skeleton was adapted from the course's Volere template. The team
retains the original headings except where changes clarify the capstone
scope. In particular, Section~\ref{sec:terminology} includes a new
symbolic-constants register; Section~\ref{sec:functional} separates
learning activities, formal specification, creative drawing, attempt
records and progress summaries; and Section~\ref{sec:changes} records
likely and unlikely changes. Section~\ref{sec:traceability} provides
cross-document traceability, while Appendix~\ref{app:sources} records
requirement evidence. A separate Hazard Analysis covers risks, failures
and mitigation measures rather than repeating Volere's risks material.
The source register includes anonymized interview paraphrases with
speaker codes and timestamps, and questionnaire statements identified by
respondent and question code. The de-identified interview transcript is
reproduced in Appendix~\ref{app:interview}, providing direct links to the
supporting interview turns (\srcref{005}).

\paragraph{Conventions and requirement format.}
Project goals use \texttt{G.1}--\texttt{G.6}; constraints use
\texttt{CON-\#\#\#}; facts \texttt{FACT-\#\#\#}; business rules
\texttt{BR-\#\#\#}; assumptions \texttt{ASM-\#\#\#}; and fictional
personas \texttt{PER-\#}. Requirement sources use
\texttt{SRC-\#\#\#}; numbered statements within a source use
\texttt{SRC-\#\#\#.\#\#}; and interview participants use anonymized
codes \texttt{P1}, \texttt{P2} and \texttt{P3}. Product use cases use
\texttt{UC-\#\#\#}; open questions use \texttt{OI-\#\#}; and likely
and unlikely changes use \texttt{LC-\#\#\#} and
\texttt{ULC-\#\#\#}, respectively.
Functional requirements have the prefixes \texttt{FR-LRN},
\texttt{FR-CRT}, \texttt{FR-REC} and \texttt{FR-PRG}, each followed by a
three-digit suffix. Nonfunctional requirements use their section's
assigned prefix. Hazard Analysis uses owner-prefixed identifiers such as
\texttt{H-AHM}, likewise followed by a three-digit suffix.
Identifiers, once assigned, are not renumbered. Each
requirement contains a short title, one testable ``shall'' statement,
rationale where needed, a fit criterion, a priority, a phase, evidence
sources and links to related use cases, requirements or hazards.
\emph{Must}, \emph{Should} and \emph{Could} identify delivery priority.
Delivery phases are \emph{PH-1} (PoC demo: Nov.~23, 2026),
\emph{PH-2} (Rev~0: Feb.~1, 2027), \emph{PH-3} (final demo:
Mar.~15, 2027), and \emph{Future}. These phase identifiers are
distinct from the interview participant codes; the requirement-by-phase
plan belongs in Section~\ref{sec:tasks}.
Symbolic constants are written in monospaced capitals (for example,
\texttt{STAGE\_PASS}) and defined once in
Section~\ref{sec:constants}. A value marked \emph{TBD} is not an
approved threshold; unresolved values appear in the constants register,
and are also linked to an open issue in Section~\ref{sec:open-issues}
where such an issue exists. Diagrams, if used, must include a legend
identifying their symbols and arrow meanings.
Section~\ref{sec:fr-formal} defines the state-machine notation used to
specify guided-lesson behaviour.

\section{Purpose of the Project}
\label{sec:purpose}

This section states the problem Squigglr addresses, who has it, and the
goals the project commits to. The problem and goals come from the Problem
Statement and Goals (PSG) document (SRC-001).

\subsection{User Business}

Visual-motor integration is the ability to coordinate what a person sees
with how they move. Children rely on it when drawing, copying shapes and
learning to write (SRC-001). Kindergarten and primary school learners need
chances to practise these skills at a suitable level and to complete
activities with less help over time. Older children who need continued
support, including some children with autism, attention-deficit/%
hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) or other developmental or learning
differences, share this need. A diagnosis alone does not determine the
support a child requires (SRC-001).

The adults around these children have a related problem. Parents,
guardians, caregivers and educators need to understand which difficulties a
child met and how much guidance the child needed. A finished drawing alone
does not show whether the child completed it independently, relied on
tracing guides, or corrected mistakes during the attempt (SRC-001).
Squigglr is a website, used on a touchscreen device with a stylus, that
offers guided drawing practice, creative drawing, and records
that help adults understand that practice. It makes no diagnosis or
treatment claims (SRC-001).

The gap left by current alternatives, as the PSG frames it, is that a
drawing's final result is kept without the process behind it: the guidance
used, the difficulties met and the corrections made. Squigglr's value is
in keeping that process visible to supporting adults while children
practise with gradually less support. The comparison with existing products
appears in the Off-the-Shelf Solutions section of this document.

\subsection{Goals of the Project}

Table~\ref{tab:goals} lists the six project goals, G.1 to G.6, using the
PSG's goal titles (SRC-001). Each goal is
stated as Purpose, Advantage and Measurement: what the goal is, the benefit
it brings and to whom, and how the team will check that it is met. Following
the teaching assistant's (TA's) request for measurable goals (SRC-003) and the supervisor's
direction that no requirements are gathered from children (SRC-004), every
check is done by the team or by adult reviewers using prepared or
adult-made attempts, never with child participants. The acceptance
parameters are named in Section~\ref{sec:constants}. On the supervisor's
advice (SRC-008), parameters that depend on the child are selected by a
supporting adult within specified ranges; tests check that the selected
settings and defaults are respected.
Each lesson (for example, drawing a square) moves through fixed stages
that give gradually less help: a demonstration, tracing a full outline,
drawing between endpoints only, and drawing with no guide. This sequence
follows the activity the team presented to the domain specialists
(SRC-005, \intref{Moustafa}{6:03}).

G.3 was revised after the PSG was submitted. The submitted PSG said
Squigglr adjusts guidance and difficulty automatically. Following the
domain-specialist interview (SRC-005, \intref{P1}{28:37}; \intref{P2}{29:18})
and the Oct.~7 TA discussion (SRC-006), the team assigned \emph{lesson
selection, starting-stage selection and discretionary stage changes} to a
supporting adult (G.3). A passing drawing attempt still advances
automatically to the next stage \emph{within the lesson the adult selected}
(G.2, BR-002); completing a lesson never automatically selects another
lesson.

G.3 Let supporting adults control support and difficulty &
\emph{Purpose:} adults choose which lesson a child practises and may let
the child start at a later stage. Changing to another lesson, skipping a
stage or moving backward requires an adult's authorization; routine forward
advancement on a passing attempt within the chosen lesson does not (G.2).

\subsection{Hands-On Users of the Project}

Squigglr has two groups of hands-on users, both primary stakeholders:
children, who complete drawing activities, and supporting adults, who choose activities and review
records.

\paragraph{Children.} Kindergarten and primary school learners, and older
children who need continued support, including children with autism, ADHD,
or other developmental or learning differences (SRC-001). The intended age
range is not yet defined; it is recorded as an open item, \texttt{AGE\_RANGE}
(TBD). Their characteristics vary widely, and a diagnosis alone does not
determine the support a child needs (SRC-001; \intref{Rayan}{2:04}).
The characteristics below matter most for the requirements, because they
shape how children read, draw and move through the application without
help:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Reading ability:} some children cannot read yet, or may
  not understand written instructions. Squigglr has to communicate in ways
  that do not depend on reading, such as images and icons (SRC-005, \intref{P1}{34:13}) and spoken audio that can be muted (SRC-005, \intref{P2}{37:39}).
  \item \textbf{Motor skills:} fine-motor ability varies; some children find holding
  a stylus difficult, and grip can affect how they draw (SRC-005, \intref{P3}{25:36}). Fine-motor control is part of visual-motor practice, so children
  need to practise holding a tool, moving from larger tools to smaller
  ones closer to what schools use (SRC-005, \intref{P1}{26:46}). Squigglr
  therefore uses a stylus as its main input. Drawing with a finger is the

  stretch goal. Some children may also have gross-motor or vision
  challenges (SRC-005, \intref{P2}{19:05}).
  \item \textbf{Support needs:} once an adult starts a lesson, he should be
  able to practise without constant adult help (SRC-005, \intref{P1}{34:13}).
  He does not need to navigate, exit or restart a lesson. Adults decide when a child moves to a new lesson
  or skips a stage (SRC-005, \intref{P1}{28:37}).
  \item \textbf{Device experience:} many young children already know how
  to navigate touchscreen applications on tablets (SRC-005, \intref{P1}{34:13}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Personas}

The three personas below are \textbf{fictional}. They do not describe any
real child or adult. Personas are used because
requirements cannot be gathered from children directly, and the supervisor
recommended them for this case (SRC-004). There are at most three, and each is a short life story of a person. Details
that come from a source cite it; all other details were invented by the
team to make the persona concrete. No information was collected from children (SRC-004). Each
persona describes one individual; none represents all children or adults
in its group.

The personas describe people, not the product. They leave out design
choices such as screen layout or feedback wording, so they stay valid if
those choices change. Each persona has a fixed identifier (PER-1 to PER-3)
so that scenarios, use cases, usability tests and later documents can
refer to it. The three personas are linked: Adam is Sarah's son and one of
Janice's learners, so one scenario can follow him between home and school. Invented details should be checked with caregivers and educators when
the opportunity arises; if one changes, the scenarios that depend on it
must be reviewed.

\paragraph{PER-1 (learner): Adam.}
Adam is 15 years old; older children who need continued support are
in scope (SRC-001). He lives with his parents and no
siblings. Adam is autistic and has ADHD. He
loves Spider-Man, colours Spider-Man pictures at home, and tends to fixate
on things he enjoys.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Communication:} he is verbal but speaks in short, simple
  sentences, and most of his responses are memorized. He can say how he
  feels or what he wants, such as being hungry or wanting to play, but
  cannot hold the conversations typical for his age. He understands
  spoken words and instructions well.
  \item \textbf{Reading and writing:} he reads and writes only with a
  parent's support. He can read a few short words he recognizes, such as
  ``yes'' and ``no'', but not longer words or sentences.
  \item \textbf{Learning:} he learns best by watching someone do a task
  first and then copying it, rather than being told what to do without an
  example.
  \item \textbf{Attention:} he loses focus easily. For skill-building
  tasks such as reading or drawing, a caregiver needs to watch over him
  throughout. When he colours, which he enjoys, he can work alone for a
  while, but needs encouragement every now and then to stay focused and
  finish.
  \item \textbf{Frustrations:} he becomes agitated when a task is repeated
  over and over.
  \item \textbf{Attitude to technology and tools:} he knows how to use an
  iPad. He has no experience with a stylus, but uses crayons, pens,
  pencils, coloured pencils and markers.
  \item \textbf{Goals:} finish a picture of something he likes and show
  it to his family.
  \item \textbf{Context of use:} at home with his mother nearby, and at
  school in sessions with Janice.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{PER-2 (caregiver): Sarah.}
Sarah is Adam's mother and works full time. She wants Adam
to keep building the drawing and fine-motor skills that support writing. She is not a specialist and learns about Adam's needs from
his teachers and therapists.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Attitude to technology:} an everyday web user who wants
  things to work without setup.
  \item \textbf{Goals:} let Adam practise without being called over
  constantly (SRC-005, \intref{P1}{34:13}); choose what Adam practises and decide
  when he moves on (SRC-005, \intref{P1}{28:37}).
  \item \textbf{Review habits:} looks through Adam's practice history on a
  laptop once a week to see which stages he reached and what keeps going
  wrong (SRC-001). Shares what she sees
  with Janice at parent meetings.
  \item \textbf{Concerns:} does not want Adam reduced to a single score, labelled or compared
  with other children (SRC-005, \intref{Moustafa}{6:03}); wants Adam's data kept
  private.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{PER-3 (educator): Janice.}
Janice is a teacher at a school for students with special needs who
works with several learners each day, including Adam. She
has a short slot with each learner and little time to set things up
between them.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Environment:} the school's devices are managed centrally,
  so she cannot install applications (SRC-003, SRC-004).
  \item \textbf{Attitude to technology:} comfortable with the school's web
  tools, but has no administrator rights.
  \item \textbf{Goals:} set a different activity for each learner

  observes in class (SRC-001).
  \item \textbf{Concerns:} switching between learners quickly, and making
  sure one learner's records are never shown to another.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Solution Constraints}
The supervisor, the TA and the domain specialists set the form of the
solution.

\begin{description}
\item[CON-001] Squigglr shall be a website used in a web browser.\\
\emph{Rationale:} Installing apps on school devices is difficult, and for
Canadian schools a website is much easier to adopt than an app-store app.\\
\emph{Imposed by:} supervisor and TA.\\
\emph{Fit criterion:} Squigglr opens and runs from a web address in a
browser.\\
\emph{Source:} SRC-003, SRC-004.
\item[CON-002] Squigglr shall require no installation on the user's
device.\\
\emph{Rationale:} Shared school devices are often managed by the school,
so adults may not be able to install software. Opening a web address is
enough to start.\\
\emph{Imposed by:} supervisor and TA.\\
\emph{Fit criterion:} a first-time user starts an activity without
installing any app, plug-in or browser extension.\\
\emph{Source:} SRC-003, SRC-004.
\item[CON-003] Squigglr shall work on touchscreen devices with stylus
input.\\
\emph{Rationale:} Squigglr targets visual-motor skills, which include fine
motor control. Children build that control by practising with a held tool,
moving from large tools to smaller ones like pencils (SRC-005, \intref{P1}{26:46}).
Grip varies between children and affects how they draw (SRC-005, \intref{P3}{25:36}), so finger input is kept as a stretch goal (SRC-001).\\
\emph{Imposed by:} domain specialists, within the scope agreed with the
supervisor and TA.\\
\emph{Fit criterion:} each activity can be completed with a stylus on a
touchscreen device; the device and browser combinations are TBD.\\
\emph{Source:} SRC-001, SRC-003, SRC-004, SRC-005.
\item[CON-007] Squigglr shall not select another lesson, skip a
guidance stage or move backward to an earlier stage unless a supporting
adult authorizes that change. Normal forward movement to the next stage
following a passing attempt \emph{within the selected lesson} is not an
unauthorized change (G.2, BR-002).\\
\emph{Rationale:} Choosing what a child is ready to practise requires
judgement by the adult who knows the child (SRC-005,
\intref{P1}{28:37}; \intref{P2}{29:18}). This restriction applies to
lesson selection and discretionary stage changes, not to the fixed
pass-based steps already defined for an adult-selected lesson. Squigglr
may suggest going back a stage, but an adult must accept (G.3).\\
\emph{Imposed by:} domain specialists' advice on adult choice, clarified
by the team's progression decision.\\
\emph{Fit criterion:} test that passing a tracing or endpoint stage
advances within the current lesson without an additional adult action;
passing the final stage completes that lesson without starting another.
Verify that switching lessons, skipping and returning to an earlier stage
cannot occur without an adult choice or acceptance.\\
\emph{Source:} SRC-001, SRC-005 (\intref{P1}{28:37};
\intref{P2}{29:18}), SRC-006; team decision documented in
\href{https://github.com/AHMEDELZARIA/squigglr/issues/132}{\#132}.
\end{description}

\subsection{Symbolic Constants}
\label{sec:constants}
Table~\ref{tab:goalconst} specifies the parameters used in guided lessons,
creative refinement and progress-summary verification. The six
adult-configurable parameters have an allowed range and a default value
for each learner profile (BR-008, \srcref{008}). Their defaults are
the midpoints of their respective ranges. Each configuration shall satisfy
\texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT} $>$ \texttt{DIFFICULTY\_STREAK} and
\texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT\_MAX} $\geq$ \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT}.
Drawing-stage results are expressed on a normalized scale from 0 to 100;
higher results indicate closer agreement with the stage's fixed criteria.
The result is not a measure of the child's ability (BR-006). Attempt-count
settings shall be integers, and values violating the allowed ranges or
the two cross-setting inequalities shall be rejected (BR-008).

\paragraph{Basis and verification of numerical values.}
The project team selected the initial ranges, defaults, five-point
\texttt{IMPROVE\_MARGIN} and 0--100 result scale in a team discussion on
15 October 2026 concerning issue
\href{https://github.com/AHMEDELZARIA/squigglr/issues/132}{\#132}.
These are team-selected engineering starting values, not thresholds
established by child testing or supplied numerically by the supervisor.
The supervisor's 14 October guidance (\srcref{008}) requires
adult-configurable ranges, midpoint defaults and example drawings
illustrating both ends of the \texttt{STAGE\_PASS} range. The chosen
values and the drawing-to-score interpretation are checked using
prepared or adult-made examples at the lower and upper boundaries of
each configurable range (OI-05; CON-005--006).

\section{Relevant Facts And Assumptions}
\label{sec:facts-assumptions}
This section records what is known to be true now, the rules that govern
how Squigglr may be used, and what the team believes but has not verified.
Facts and assumptions are kept apart so that a reader can see which
requirements rest on unconfirmed beliefs. The Hazard Analysis uses the
assumptions as possible causes of hazards: if an assumption turns out to
be false, the requirements that depend on it must be reviewed. Each entry identifies the goals, constraints or product behaviours it
supports; Section~\ref{sec:functional} specifies the corresponding
functional requirements.

\subsection{Relevant Facts}
\label{sec:facts}
Each fact below is true at the time of writing and is supported by a
source in Appendix~\ref{app:sources}. Facts from the interview are expert
statements, not observations of children.

\begin{description}[style=nextline]
\item[FACT-001] Visual-motor integration is linked to handwriting
legibility in kindergarten children, but the link is a correlation; it
does not show that practising one improves the other (SRC-001;
\intref{Rayan}{2:04}).\\
\emph{Relevance:} Squigglr makes no claim that its practice improves
handwriting or visual-motor skills (BR-006).
\item[FACT-002] The study of structured, graded practice cited in the
Problem Statement had no control group. It supports the idea of graded
practice but does not show that Squigglr will produce the same results
(SRC-001; \intref{Rayan}{2:04}).\\
\emph{Relevance:} effectiveness is not a goal or a fit criterion of this
project.
\item[FACT-003] Motor and handwriting difficulties occur in some autistic
children and some children with ADHD, with severity ranging from mild to
severe; not every child in these groups has them (SRC-001;
\intref{Rayan}{2:04}).\\
\emph{Relevance:} guidance and difficulty are set per child by an adult,
not per diagnosis (G.3).
\item[FACT-004] The specialists stated that deciding automatically when a
child is ready for the next level requires assessment, which is too
complex for the project's time frame, and that an adult who knows the
child should decide (SRC-005, \intref{P1}{28:37}; \intref{P2}{29:18}).\\
\emph{Relevance:} basis for adult-controlled lesson selection and
nonroutine stage changes (CON-007, G.3, BR-003); the team retains fixed
pass-based forward progression within the selected lesson (G.2).
\item[FACT-005] The specialists stated that how a child holds a tool
affects how they draw, that finger input is the easiest start, and that
fine-motor practice needs a held tool, moving from large to small
(SRC-005, \intref{P3}{25:36}; \intref{P1}{26:46}).\\
\emph{Relevance:} basis for stylus input (CON-003) with finger input as a
stretch goal.
\item[FACT-006] The specialists stated that tracing in the air in front
of a camera practises gross rather than fine motor skills, and that it
should come after the touchscreen activities when time is limited
(SRC-005, \intref{P1}{13:42}; \intref{P1}{16:13}; \intref{P2}{16:45}).\\
\emph{Relevance:} camera finger tracing stays a stretch goal.
\item[FACT-007] The specialists stated that young children may not read
yet but navigate games through images and icons, and that a child should
be able to enter a lesson, practise, restart and move back and forth
without constant adult help, while adults handle setup (SRC-005,
\intref{P1}{34:13}).\\
\emph{Relevance:} G.1 and the usability requirements; requirement IDs to
be linked when written.
\item[FACT-008] A study that involves children requires ethics approval;
meeting adult experts for feedback, without involving children, does not
(SRC-004; SRC-005, \intref{P2}{22:56}; \intref{P1}{33:00}).\\
\emph{Relevance:} basis for CON-005 and CON-006.
\item[FACT-009] Installing applications on school-managed devices is
difficult, and a website is easier for Canadian schools to adopt (SRC-003,
SRC-004).\\
\emph{Relevance:} basis for CON-001 and CON-002.
\item[FACT-010] The specialists recommended starting from the Web Content
Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG), which one participant stated are adopted
in law in many countries, and then applying design heuristics for autism
from the literature (SRC-005, \intref{P2}{39:26}).\\
\emph{Relevance:} accessibility and standards requirements; requirement
IDs to be linked when written.
\item[FACT-011] The course fixes the project's deadlines (CON-004,
SRC-002).\\
\emph{Relevance:} scope decisions, such as keeping assessment and camera
tracing out of the core product, follow from the time available.
\item[FACT-012] External requirements evidence includes a 43-minute
interview with three domain specialists and a follow-up questionnaire
answered by two of them. No parent, educator, school administrator or
occupational therapist has been consulted, and no child has taken part
in any source (SRC-005, SRC-009; \srcref{004}).\\
\emph{Relevance:} requirements based on what parents and educators need
rest on assumptions (ASM-006) until those groups are consulted.
\item[FACT-013] The interview did not cover several topics that affect
the requirements: how Squigglr compares with existing drawing apps (one
participant noted only that many exist (SRC-005, \intref{P2}{35:36})),
differences between home and school use, the
relative priority of the features, and how practice records should be
kept, shared or deleted (SRC-005).\\
The follow-up questionnaire later covered refinement in the creative space
and letter activities (SRC-009).\\
\emph{Relevance:} the requirements on these topics rest on assumptions
(ASM-010 to ASM-012) and are open to change.
\end{description}

\subsection{Business Rules}
\label{sec:business-rules}
The rules below govern who may do what with Squigglr and what Squigglr
must never do. They hold no matter how Squigglr is designed. Each rule has
an identifier of the form BR-\#\#\#, a rationale and its sources.

\begin{description}[style=nextline]
\item[BR-001] Only the adult who holds an account, a parent or an
educator, may create, view and manage the learner profiles under it and
their records. One account can hold several learner profiles. A learner
profile holds only the child's first name and a username that does not
identify the child; no other information about the child is collected.\\
\emph{Rationale:} practice records describe a child's difficulties and
are personal information. Keeping each profile to a first name and a
non-identifying username lets an adult tell children apart, for example on
a shared device, while collecting as little about them as possible. How
records are protected is a privacy requirement.\\
\emph{Source:} SRC-001, SRC-004; G.5, G.6.
\item[BR-002] Within an adult-selected lesson, Squigglr automatically
moves the child to the next guidance stage when an attempt meets the
current drawing stage's pass criteria (\texttt{STAGE\_PASS}). Passing
the independent drawing stage completes the lesson; it does not select or
start another lesson. After \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT} (larger than
\texttt{DIFFICULTY\_STREAK}) unsuccessful attempts at one stage, the lesson
ends, and the furthest stage reached and the number of successful attempts
at each stage are saved. If, when \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT} is reached, the
average result of the last \texttt{IMPROVE\_WINDOW} attempts is better
than that of the \texttt{IMPROVE\_WINDOW} attempts before them by at least
\texttt{IMPROVE\_MARGIN}, the child is improving and Squigglr grants a single extension of up to
\texttt{ATTEMPT\_EXTENSION} additional evaluable attempts. The extension
cannot be granted a second time at the same stage, regardless of later
result changes. Total evaluable attempts at a stage never exceed
\texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT\_MAX}.\\
\emph{Rationale:} moving forward on a pass checks the drawing against fixed
criteria and does not assess the child (G.2). Ending the lesson stops a
child from repeating a stage they cannot pass without limit, and saving the
progress lets the adult decide what comes next. Extending the limit while
the child improves avoids cutting off practice that is working, and
\texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT\_MAX} keeps the stop. The comparison uses only the
child's own attempts against the stage's fixed criteria, so it does not
assess the child.\\
\emph{Source:} G.2, G.3, G.5.
\item[BR-003] A supporting adult chooses which lesson the child practises
and its starting stage. Squigglr never changes lessons, skips a stage or
moves back a stage on its own. Passing an attempt may advance the child
to the next stage \emph{within that same lesson} as defined by BR-002. After
\texttt{DIFFICULTY\_STREAK} failed attempts in a row, Squigglr may
suggest going back a stage, unless the child is improving (BR-002); the change happens only if an adult accepts.\\
\emph{Rationale:} deciding readiness needs assessment that is out of
scope (FACT-004), and the adult knows the child's level.\\
\emph{Source:} CON-007; G.3; SRC-005, \intref{P1}{28:37};
\intref{P2}{29:18}.
\item[BR-004] A guided lesson record keeps the child's actual strokes.
Squigglr never replaces them with a corrected or ideal shape.\\
\emph{Rationale:} adults judge progress from the records; a record that
shows a corrected shape would mislead them.\\
\emph{Source:} G.5; SRC-005, \intref{Moustafa}{6:03}.
\item[BR-005] Refinement of strokes happens only in the creative space.
It is automatic and minimal, the refined drawing stays editable, and the
child's original drawing can be restored. Refinement can also be turned
off for the creative space. Restoring and turning off are done by the child
or, if the child cannot, by the adult.\\
\emph{Rationale:} the work must remain the child's own, and refinement
must never reach the records adults rely on (BR-004). Refinement needs no
action from the child, and an adult can restore the original or turn
refinement off for a child who cannot use the controls. Both questionnaire
respondents suggested that refinement be optional or adjustable
(\hyperref[src:009.01]{SRC-009.01}).\\
\emph{Source:} G.4; SRC-001; \hyperref[src:009.01]{SRC-009.01}.
\item[BR-006] Squigglr gives no diagnosis, no judgement of a child's
abilities, no comparison with other children, and no claim that its
practice improves any skill.\\
\emph{Rationale:} the team are not occupational therapists and cannot
judge a child's abilities (SRC-008), and the evidence does not support
effectiveness claims (FACT-001, FACT-002).\\
\emph{Source:} SRC-001, SRC-008; SRC-005, \intref{Moustafa}{6:03}.
\item[BR-007] Feedback during a guided activity comes from observations
the team defines in advance, such as a missed part of an outline. It is
not personalized advice generated by an AI model.\\
\emph{Rationale:} predefined observations can be reviewed and checked
before release; generated advice to a child cannot.\\
\emph{Source:} SRC-001; SRC-005, \intref{Moustafa}{6:03}.
\item[BR-008] The supporting adult sets \texttt{STAGE\_PASS},
\texttt{DIFFICULTY\_STREAK}, \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT},
\texttt{IMPROVE\_WINDOW}, \texttt{ATTEMPT\_EXTENSION} and
\texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT\_MAX} for each child.
When an adult creates a child profile, Squigglr asks them to choose each
value, and for \texttt{STAGE\_PASS} shows example drawings of what its
minimum and maximum look like. The adult can change the values later. A value the adult does not
choose is set to the middle of its range. Squigglr accepts only values
within the range, only an \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT} larger than
\texttt{DIFFICULTY\_STREAK}, and only an \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT\_MAX} at
least \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT}.\\
\emph{Rationale:} the adult knows the child, and a value that suits one
child may not suit another (FACT-003). The team sets only the minimum and
maximum, so no single value has to be shown right for every child.\\
\emph{Source:} SRC-008; CON-007; Table~\ref{tab:goalconst}.
\item[BR-009] The team sets the minimum and maximum of each adult-set
value, and the fixed values in Table~\ref{tab:goalconst}, using
adult-made drawings, never from testing with children.\\
\emph{Rationale:} no study with children takes place (CON-005, CON-006).\\
\emph{Source:} SRC-004, SRC-008; Table~\ref{tab:goalconst}.
\end{description}

\subsection{Assumptions}
\label{sec:assumptions}
The assumptions below are beliefs the team holds but has not verified.
Each has an identifier of the form ASM-\#\#\#, a rationale, what depends
on it, and the effect if it is false. Assumptions marked as relevant to
safety are inputs to the Hazard Analysis.

\begin{description}[style=nextline]
\item[ASM-001] An adult is present when a child starts a guided lesson, to
choose the lesson and the starting stage.\\
\emph{Rationale:} the specialists described adults handling setup and
deciding progression (SRC-005, \intref{P1}{28:37}; \intref{P1}{34:13}).
How often this happens at home or in class is unknown.\\
\emph{Depends on it:} G.3, BR-003, CON-007.\\
\emph{If false:} a child may start at a level an adult did not choose.
Relevant to safety.
\item[ASM-002] A child may continue practising without an adult watching,
but an adult nearby remains responsible for the child's physical safety,
wellbeing and time on the device. Squigglr cannot observe or prevent
physical harm, distress or overuse.\\
\emph{Rationale:} the specialists advised that children should practise
without constant adult help (SRC-005, \intref{P1}{34:13}), and the
Problem Statement expects adult support as each learner needs (SRC-001).\\
\emph{Depends on it:} G.1, the safety requirements (requirement IDs to be
linked when written).\\
\emph{If false:} the safety requirements must cover unsupervised use,
for example limits on session length or sound. Relevant to safety.
\item[ASM-003] Homes and schools that use Squigglr have a touchscreen
device with a stylus that works in a supported browser.\\
\emph{Rationale:} the stylus is the main input (CON-003), and the
interview example used a tablet with a stylus (SRC-005,
\intref{Rayan}{24:56}). Which devices families and schools own is not
known. The supported device and browser combinations are TBD.\\
\emph{Depends on it:} CON-003, G.1, G.2.\\
\emph{If false:} children without a stylus can still use Squigglr with a
finger, but not as intended: the stage checks and value ranges are set for
stylus input, and tuning them for finger input is a stretch goal.
\item[ASM-004] Supported browsers report stylus strokes in enough detail
(position and order of points) for Squigglr to check an attempt against
\texttt{STAGE\_PASS} and to store the actual strokes.\\
\emph{Rationale:} the stage checks and the records both depend on the
strokes; this has not been tested on any device yet.\\
\emph{Depends on it:} G.2, G.5, BR-004.\\
\emph{If false:} an attempt may be judged wrongly or stored inaccurately,
which would mislead the adult. The team may then need to deliver Squigglr
as an installed app, if input there is more reliable, which would change
CON-001. Relevant to safety.
\item[ASM-005] A network connection is available during normal use, for
loading activities and for saving and viewing records. Offline use is out
of the initial scope.\\
\emph{Rationale:} stated in the Problem Statement (SRC-001); school and
home networks have not been checked.\\
\emph{Depends on it:} G.5, G.6, CON-001.\\
\emph{If false:} attempts may be lost when the connection drops, and an
adult may decide from incomplete records. Relevant to safety.
\item[ASM-006] The supporting adult can judge from the records when a
child should move on, stay or go back.\\
\emph{Rationale:} the specialists placed this decision with a parent or
educator who knows the child (SRC-005, \intref{P1}{28:37}). Squigglr
shows the adult every value it records, such as each attempt's result and
the \texttt{STAGE\_PASS} and \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT} in use for that
attempt, and the adult chooses which of them their view shows, so the team
does not have to pick which measures matter.\\
\emph{Depends on it:} G.3, G.6, CON-007, BR-003.\\
\emph{If false:} a child may be kept at a level that is too hard or too
easy; the adult views would need more guidance. Relevant to
safety.
\item[ASM-007] The adult who uses the adult views can read the language of
the interface. The interface language is TBD. Children do not need to
read to use the child views.\\
\emph{Rationale:} adult tasks such as reviewing records need text, while
child navigation uses images and icons (FACT-007).\\
\emph{Depends on it:} G.6 and the usability requirements (requirement IDs
to be linked when written).\\
\emph{If false:} adults may misread records or settings.
\item[ASM-008] On a shared device, an adult makes sure the right child
is selected before a session and that another child cannot open the
previous child's records.\\
\emph{Rationale:} school devices may be shared by several children
(SRC-004). One adult account can hold several learner profiles (BR-001).\\
\emph{Depends on it:} BR-001, G.5.\\
\emph{If false:} attempts may be saved to the wrong child, or a child's
records may be seen by others. Relevant to safety and privacy.
\item[ASM-009] The adults who use Squigglr for a child have the right to
see that child's records, and any consent this needs is obtained outside
Squigglr.\\
\emph{Rationale:} Squigglr cannot verify a parent's or school's authority
over a child; it only knows the adult who holds the account (BR-001).\\
\emph{Depends on it:} BR-001, the privacy requirements (requirement IDs to
be linked when written).\\
\emph{If false:} an unauthorised adult could see a child's records.
Relevant to privacy.
\item[ASM-010] Lines, basic shapes and simple drawings are suitable
practice content for the intended children, without letters.\\
\emph{Rationale:} the specialists did not object to this content, and
nurseries focus on these skills (SRC-005, \intref{P1}{20:27}). Two
specialists saw letters as a later stage after shapes (SRC-009.04), so
they remain a stretch goal (SRC-001).\\
\emph{Depends on it:} G.1, G.2.\\
\emph{If false:} the activity set changes.
\item[ASM-011] Children and adults value automatic refinement in the
creative space, and children understand that the refined drawing is still
theirs.\\
\emph{Rationale:} questionnaire respondents supported subtle, optional
refinement that preserves the child's drawing (SRC-009.01), but their
responses do not establish children's or caregivers' preferences.\\
\emph{Depends on it:} G.4, BR-005.\\
\emph{If false:} refinement may discourage a child, and G.4 would need
to change.
\item[ASM-012] Squigglr serves the same needs at home and in school, so
one set of requirements covers both.\\
\emph{Rationale:} the specialists named homes, kindergartens, schools and
rehabilitation centres as possible settings (SRC-005,
\intref{P2}{21:25}) but did not discuss differences between them
(FACT-013).\\
\emph{Depends on it:} G.3, G.6, BR-001.\\
\emph{If false:} separate requirements may be needed, for example for an
educator managing several children.
\item[ASM-013] Schools allow access to the Squigglr website on their
managed devices and networks.\\
\emph{Rationale:} a website is easier to adopt than an installed app
(FACT-009), but no school has confirmed that it would not block the site.\\
\emph{Depends on it:} CON-001, CON-002.\\
\emph{If false:} school use needs approval from administrators.
\item[ASM-014] Strokes drawn by adults, or prepared examples, are close
enough to children's drawing to verify stage checks and refinement.\\
\emph{Rationale:} evaluation uses adults only (CON-005, CON-006).\\
\emph{Depends on it:} G.2, G.4, BR-009, and the verification plan.\\
\emph{If false:} the range of \texttt{STAGE\_PASS} and the value of
\texttt{REFINE\_TOLERANCE} may be wrong for children, so attempts may be
judged wrongly. Relevant to safety.
\item[ASM-015] An adult can turn off audio feedback, or stop a session,
if sound or on-screen feedback upsets a child.\\
\emph{Rationale:} the specialists recommended that users can turn audio
off (SRC-005, \intref{P2}{37:39}). Children react to sensory feedback in
different ways, and the team has no information on which feedback upsets
which children.\\
\emph{Depends on it:} the accessibility and safety requirements
(requirement IDs to be linked when written).\\
\emph{If false:} feedback must be safe by default rather than relying on
an adult. Relevant to safety.
\item[ASM-016] A supporting adult can choose adult-set values
(Table~\ref{tab:goalconst}) that suit their child, helped by the example
drawings for \texttt{STAGE\_PASS}, or leave the defaults.\\
\emph{Rationale:} the supervisor advised leaving these values to the adult
(SRC-008); no adult has tried the choice yet.\\
\emph{Depends on it:} G.2, G.3, BR-008.\\
\emph{If false:} a child may be held at a stage too long or moved on too
soon, which can frustrate them. Relevant to safety.
\item[ASM-017] A rise in the average result of a child's recent attempts
by at least \texttt{IMPROVE\_MARGIN} reflects real improvement, not chance
variation between drawings.\\
\emph{Rationale:} drawing results vary from one attempt to the next; the
team will check the margin with prepared adult-made sequences, not with
children (CON-005, CON-006).\\
\emph{Depends on it:} G.3, BR-002.\\
\emph{If false:} a lesson may be extended for a child who is not
improving, or not extended for one who is. \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT\_MAX}
still ends the lesson. Relevant to safety.
\end{description}

\section{The Scope of the Work}
\label{sec:work-scope}

This section describes the work that Squigglr will become part of:
children practising drawing, and the adults around them choosing that
practice and following the child's progress. It covers how the work is done
now, the boundary of the work and what crosses it, the business events the
work responds to, and the business use case for each event. Squigglr's own
part of the work is in Section~\ref{sec:product-scope}.

\subsection{The Current Situation}
\label{sec:current-situation}

Children practise drawing lines, shapes and simple drawings at home, in
nurseries and kindergartens, in schools and in rehabilitation centres
(SRC-005, \intref{P1}{20:27}; \intref{P2}{21:25}). They practise with tools
they hold, starting with large ones that are easier for their hands and
moving to smaller ones, such as pencils, that are closer to what they will
use in school (SRC-005, \intref{P1}{26:46}). Some children also take part in
therapy programmes run by occupational therapists (SRC-005,
\intref{P1}{32:42}). Structured programmes exist that grade the same
activity into easier and harder levels (SRC-001), and many drawing
applications are available for children (SRC-005, \intref{P2}{35:36}). The
comparison with existing products is in the Off-the-Shelf Solutions
section.

The adults who support a child follow progress mainly through what the
child produces. A finished drawing does not show whether the child made it
alone, relied on tracing guides, or corrected mistakes along the way
(SRC-001). The adult who knows the child decides when the child is ready to
move on (FACT-004). The work as it is done now therefore has three gaps
that the project goals address:
\begin{itemize}
  \item practice is not always graded so that help fades as the child
  improves (\goalref{1}, \goalref{2});
  \item the adult's decision on the next step rests on finished drawings,
  not on a record of how they were made (\goalref{3}, \goalref{5});
  \item nothing summarizes the child's practice over time for the adult
  (\goalref{6}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Specifying a Business Use Case (BUC)}
\label{sec:buc}

Each business use case below is the work's response to the business event
with the same number. It describes the whole response, including the parts
done by people, and names the product use cases (Table~\ref{tab:puc}) that
are Squigglr's part of it.

\begin{description}[style=nextline]
\item[BUC-001 Add a child (BE-001)] The adult creates a profile for the
child and chooses each adult-set value, using the example drawings for
\texttt{STAGE\_PASS}, or leaves the defaults (BR-008). The work records
the profile and the values. Product use case: \ucref{001}.
\item[BUC-002 Choose the next practice (BE-002)] The adult judges what
the child is ready for, from knowing the child and, once there is
practice, from the records (ASM-006). The adult then chooses a lesson and
its starting stage. Product use cases: \ucref{002}, \ucref{004}.
\item[BUC-003 Practise a guided lesson (BE-003)] The adult starts the
lesson. The child watches the demonstration and draws an attempt at each
stage. The work checks each attempt against the stage's criteria, gives
feedback, and moves the child on or offers a retry. The lesson ends when
the child finishes it or reaches \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT}, or
\texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT\_MAX} when extended, and every attempt is recorded
(BR-002).
Product use cases: \ucref{003}, \ucref{008}.
\item[BUC-004 Respond to repeated difficulty (BE-004)] The work suggests
going back a stage. The adult decides whether to accept; a declined or
ignored suggestion changes nothing (BR-003). Product use case:
\ucref{005}.
\item[BUC-005 Draw freely (BE-005)] The child draws and edits in the
creative space. The work refines the drawing a little and keeps it
editable. The child or the adult can turn refinement off or restore the
original (BR-005; SRC-009.01). Product use cases: \ucref{006}, \ucref{007}.
\item[BUC-006 Review progress (BE-006)] The adult reads a summary of the
child's practice, opens the attempts behind it, and uses what they see
to decide on further practice (BUC-002). The work gives no diagnosis or
judgement of the child (BR-006). Product use case: \ucref{009}.
\item[BUC-007 Change a child's settings (BE-007)] The adult opens the
child's history or settings and changes accessibility, audio or
adult-set values. The work accepts only values within their ranges
(BR-008). Product use case: \ucref{010}.
\item[BUC-008 Maintain lessons and ranges (BE-008)] The team adds or
changes lessons, or changes the ranges of the adult-set values, using
adult-made drawings (BR-009). Squigglr has no product use case for this
event; the maintenance requirements cover it.
\end{description}

\section{Business Data Model and Data Dictionary}
\label{sec:data-model}

This section defines the business data that Squigglr creates, uses and
keeps: what a supporting adult's account, a learner profile, a lesson,
a lesson record, an attempt record and a creative drawing are, how they relate, and what
each term in the requirements means. The model is
conceptual. Following the Volere template~\cite{robertson2012volere}, it
records the subject matter the business must remember and the rules that hold between its parts; it
is not a database design, and it fixes no tables, keys, storage or
technology. The names defined here are the names the functional
requirements use, in particular those of
Sections~\ref{sec:fr-creative}, \ref{sec:fr-records} and
\ref{sec:fr-progress}, and the product use cases UC-001 to UC-010
(Table~\ref{tab:puc}) create, read and change this data.
Subsection~\ref{sec:bdm} gives the model as a diagram and states the data
constraints. Subsection~\ref{sec:data-dictionary} defines every class,
attribute and data flow.

\subsection{Business Data Model}
\label{sec:bdm}

Figure~\ref{fig:data-model} shows the seven classes of business data that
Squigglr keeps and how they relate. A supporting adult's account holds
learner profiles, and each learner profile has its own learner settings
(BR-001, BR-008). The team maintains the lessons, each with its four
stages and their predefined difficulties (BR-009; LC-001 in
Table~\ref{tab:likelyChanges}). Each time an adult starts a lesson for a
learner, Squigglr keeps a new lesson record, which holds the attempt
records and any go-back suggestions of that lesson (G.3, G.5). The
creative space keeps creative drawings, each with its strokes and
refinements (G.4, BR-005). A progress summary is not a class: Squigglr
derives it from one learner's lesson and attempt records whenever an adult
opens it (G.6), so Table~\ref{tab:data-dictionary} defines it as a data
flow. That table also gives the attributes of each class and how many of
one class relate to another.

The model carries the following data constraints. None is new; each
restates as a property of the data a rule or goal stated elsewhere, and
each can be checked by inspecting saved data prepared by adults.
\begin{itemize}
  \item A learner profile holds only the child's first name and a
  username that does not identify the child (BR-001).
  \item Every adult-set value lies within its team-set range,
  \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT} is larger than \texttt{DIFFICULTY\_STREAK},
  and \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT\_MAX} is at least \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT}; a
  value the adult does not choose is the middle of its range (BR-008).
  \item Within one lesson record, the evaluable attempts at one stage
  never exceed \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT\_MAX} (BR-002). An interrupted
  attempt, or one whose input is invalid or too ambiguous to evaluate,
  is not an evaluable attempt and is not counted (FR-LRN-009,
  FR-LRN-010).
  \item The actual strokes of an attempt record are the strokes the child
  drew; they are never replaced by a corrected, ideal or refined shape
  (BR-004, BR-005).
  \item Every observed difficulty of an attempt record is a predefined
  difficulty of that attempt's stage, and a later correction adds a mark
  without removing the difficulty (G.5, BR-007).
  \item A refinement keeps every refined stroke within
  \texttt{REFINE\_TOLERANCE} of the original stroke, and it changes
  neither the number nor the order, colours and widths of the strokes
  (G.4, BR-005; FR-CRT-011, FR-CRT-012).
  \item The original strokes of a refinement never change, so a restore
  returns them exactly (G.4, BR-005).
  \item A creative drawing is never an attempt record and never counts in
  a lesson record or a progress summary (BR-004; FR-CRT-028).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{UC-003: Practise a guided lesson}
\label{puc:003}
\reqfield{Actor}{Child.}
\reqfield{Preconditions}{An adult has started the lesson for the child
(\ucref{002} or \ucref{004}).}
\reqfield{Trigger}{An adult starts a lesson for the child.}
\noindent\textit{Main flow:}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep, leftmargin=2.2em]
  \item Squigglr shows the demonstration, which is not an attempt
  (\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-003}}), then moves to the starting stage.
  \item Squigglr shows the stage's guidance: the full outline, the
  endpoints only, or no guide
  (\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-004}}--\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-006}}).
  \item The child draws one or more strokes. Lifting the stylus does not
  end the attempt (\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-007}},
  \mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-008}}).
  \item When the attempt is finalized, Squigglr classifies it as complete
  or incomplete and as assisted or independent, and passes it only if it
  is complete and meets \texttt{STAGE\_PASS}
  (\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-011}}--\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-013}}).
  \item Squigglr shows pre-approved feedback about the drawing, never
  about the child (\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-014}},
  \mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-022}}), and records the attempt (\ucref{008}).
  \item On a pass, Squigglr moves to the next stage and repeats from
  step 2. A pass at independent drawing completes the lesson
  (\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-023}}).
\end{enumerate}
\noindent\textit{Alternative flows:}
\begin{description}[nosep, leftmargin=2.2em, font=\normalfont]
  \item[6a.] The attempt does not pass and the stage's limit is not
  reached. The child retries from step 3 (\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-015}}).
  \item[6b.] The child has \texttt{DIFFICULTY\_STREAK} non-passing
  attempts in a row and is not improving. Squigglr offers the adult a
  suggestion to go back a stage (\ucref{005}); practice continues
  meanwhile (\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-019}}, \mbox{SAF-RAY-001}).
  \item[6c.] The unsuccessful attempts reach \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT}
  while the child is improving. Squigglr allows up to
  \texttt{ATTEMPT\_EXTENSION} more attempts, never more than
  \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT\_MAX} in total, and the child continues from
  step 3 (\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-020}}).
\end{description}
\noindent\textit{Failure flows:}
\begin{description}[nosep, leftmargin=2.2em, font=\normalfont]
  \item[3a.] The attempt is interrupted or unfinished. It is not passed
  and does not count toward the limits (\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-009}}).
  \item[3b.] The input is missing, invalid, too ambiguous to evaluate, or
  cannot be mapped reliably to the drawing surface. It is not passed and
  does not count (\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-010}}, \mbox{SAF-AHM-002}).
  \item[3c.] The same stylus event is reported more than once. It counts
  once (\mbox{SAF-AHM-001}).
  \item[6d.] The unsuccessful attempts reach \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT}
  without an extension, an extension is used up, or the total reaches
  \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT\_MAX}. The lesson ends with no action from the
  child, and the progress so far is saved (\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-021}},
  \mbox{SAF-RAY-005}, \mbox{SAF-RAY-006}).
  \item[*a.] At any step, the connection is lost. Each completed attempt
  is kept on the device until the server confirms it is saved, and the
  adult is told which attempts could not be saved (\mbox{SAF-RAY-003}).
\end{description}
\reqfield{Postconditions}{Every evaluable attempt is recorded, the
furthest stage reached is saved, and no stage moved back without an
adult's acceptance.}
\reqfield{Requirements}{\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-002}}--\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-015}},
\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-019}}--\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-023}}; \mbox{SAF-RAY-003},
\mbox{SAF-RAY-005}, \mbox{SAF-RAY-006}, \mbox{SAF-RAY-007}; \mbox{SAF-AHM-001},
\mbox{SAF-AHM-002}.}
\reqfield{Sources}{\goalref{1}, \goalref{2}, \goalref{3}; \mbox{SRC-005.01}.}

\section{Functional Requirements}
\label{sec:functional}
Functional requirements are grouped by externally observable product
behaviour. Each requirement uses the identifier, fit-criterion and
traceability conventions defined in the Introduction.

\subsection{Learning Activity Requirements}
\label{sec:fr-learning}

This subsection specifies the externally observable behaviour of guided
lessons. It builds on G.1--G.3, CON-003, CON-007 and BR-002--BR-009,
with source statements in Appendix~\ref{app:sources}.
The core catalogue covers lines, shapes and simple drawings; letter tracing
is deferred (ASM-010, LC-001). Delivery-phase identifiers follow the
conventions in the Introduction and the phase plan in
Section~\ref{sec:tasks}.

\paragraph{Within-lesson progression and adult control.}
A supporting adult selects each lesson and its starting stage and authorizes
skips or backward changes (G.3, CON-007). Within the selected lesson, a
passing drawing attempt automatically advances to the next guidance stage
(G.2, BR-002). Passing the independent stage completes the current lesson;
Squigglr does not automatically select another lesson.

\paragraph{Lesson content and guidance.}
\begin{srsrequirement}{FR-LRN-001}{Approved lesson catalogue}
  \reqshall{Squigglr shall offer guided lessons for the approved catalogue of lines,
basic shapes and simple drawings, excluding letter-tracing activities from
the core catalogue.}
  \reqrationale{These are the agreed categories for structured practice; letters are deferred
(G.1, ASM-010). The lesson catalogue is maintained separately from the stage rules
(LC-001).}
  \reqfit{Inspect the adult-visible lesson catalogue and verify that every approved
catalogue entry is selectable, classified as a line, shape or simple drawing,
and has the stages in FR-LRN-002; verify that no letter activity is required
to complete the core catalogue.}
  \reqpriority{Must}
  \reqphase{PH-1}
  \reqsources{G.1; \srcref{001}; \hyperref[src:009.04]{SRC-009.04}}
  \reqtraces{ASM-010; LC-001; \ucref{002}}
\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{FR-LRN-002}{Ordered guidance stages}
  \reqshall{Squigglr shall make the following ordered guidance stages available for each
guided lesson: demonstration, full-outline tracing, endpoint-only guidance
and independent drawing.}
  \reqrationale{The activity described to specialists removes guidance in this order
(G.1--G.2).}
  \reqfit{For every approved lesson, inspect all four stages in the specified order;
confirm that the stage chosen by an adult is the stage presented when
practice begins.}
  \reqpriority{Must}
  \reqphase{PH-1}
  \reqsources{G.1--G.2; \srcref{001}; \srcref{005}}
  \reqtraces{BR-003; \ucref{003}; \ucref{004}}
\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{FR-LRN-003}{Demonstration}
  \reqshall{Squigglr shall show an example of the intended drawing during a lesson's
demonstration stage without treating that demonstration as a learner attempt.}
  \reqrationale{A demonstration provides an example before guided practice and must not
inflate the learner's records.}
  \reqfit{Start each approved lesson in its demonstration stage; verify that the
intended drawing can be viewed and that watching it adds no learner attempt
or passed-stage record.}
  \reqpriority{Must}
  \reqphase{PH-1}
  \reqsources{G.1; \srcref{001}; \srcref{005}}
  \reqtraces{BR-004; \ucref{003}; \ucref{008}}
\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{FR-LRN-004}{Full-outline guidance}
  \reqshall{Squigglr shall display the lesson's complete reference outline during the
full-outline tracing stage and accept stylus strokes drawn against it.}
  \reqrationale{Outline tracing is the first guided drawing stage in the approved
progression.}
  \reqfit{For each lesson, enter the full-outline stage, verify that the complete
reference shape is visible, and draw an attempt using stylus input.}
  \reqpriority{Must}
  \reqphase{PH-1}
  \reqsources{G.1; \srcref{001}; \srcref{005}}
  \reqtraces{CON-003; \ucref{003}}
\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{FR-LRN-005}{Endpoint-only guidance}
  \reqshall{Squigglr shall show the specified start and end points, without the full
reference outline, during the endpoint-only stage and accept stylus strokes
between those points.}
  \reqrationale{The middle drawing stage reduces visual assistance while retaining the
destination cues.}
  \reqfit{For each lesson, enter endpoint-only guidance; verify that the relevant
endpoints are shown, the full outline is absent, and stylus drawing is
accepted.}

\subsection{Formal Specification}
\label{sec:fr-formal}

The most safety-relevant learning behaviour is formalized here as a
\emph{labelled transition system}: a set of states and named events with
rules describing when an event changes the state. The model specifies
externally observable outcomes rather than a particular user interface or
scoring algorithm. It covers guidance levels, attempt classification,
optional suggestions, automatic pass-based advancement within a lesson,
and the unsuccessful-attempt stopping rule.
Every variable used below has a type or domain; arrows indicate transitions
\emph{caused by the event above the arrow}, not automatic passage of time.
This notation applies only to guided learning activities.

\paragraph{Domains and state.}
For each selected lesson $\ell$ and learner profile $p$, let
\[
\begin{aligned}
L &= \{D,T,E,I\}, & A &= \{T,E,I\},\\
R &= \{\textsf{complete},\textsf{incomplete},\textsf{invalid}\},\\
M &= \{\textsf{assisted},\textsf{independent}\},\\
Q &= \{\textsf{active},\textsf{suggested},\textsf{ended}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The levels $D,T,E,I$ mean \emph{demonstration}, full-outline
\emph{tracing}, \emph{endpoint-only} and \emph{independent} drawing,
respectively. Only levels in $A$ admit scored drawing attempts; $D$ is a
non-scored demonstration. A state is
\[
\sigma=(\ell,p,l,q,n_f,n_a,k,H,s,z,x,f),
\]
where $l\in L$ is the current guidance level of the adult-selected lesson,
$q\in Q$ is the session status, $n_f\in\mathbb{N}_0$ counts non-passing
evaluable attempts at this stage, $n_a\in\mathbb{N}_0$ counts finalized,
evaluable attempts at this stage, $k\in\mathbb{N}_0$ is the consecutive
non-passing streak, and $H=(r_1,\ldots,r_m)$ is the ordered list of
stage-result values. The flag $s\in\{0,1\}$ indicates a pending
suggestion; $z\in\{0,1\}$ indicates whether the single stage extension
has been granted; $x\in\mathbb{N}_0$ counts evaluable attempts consumed
\emph{after} that grant, with $0\le x\le e_p$ and $x=0$ when $z=0$.
Finally, $f\in L$ is the furthest stage reached in the lesson.
On a stage change (either normal pass-based progression or an
authorized adult adjustment), the stage-specific counts, streak, history,
pending suggestion and extension state reset to zero or empty; $f$ retains
the furthest stage reached so far. \emph{No child ability estimate} is
part of this state (BR-006).

\paragraph{Drawing input and attempt classification.}
An attempt is a finite ordered list of stylus strokes
$X=(x_1,\ldots,x_j)$, where every $x_i$ is an ordered list of
reported points. Lifting the stylus closes one $x_i$, not the attempt.
The terminal event $\operatorname{Finalize}(X)$ denotes the end of input
collection for an attempt. This abstract event does not prescribe a
particular user-interface control. A stylus lift alone does not finalize
the attempt; interruption before finalization cannot produce a passed
result (FR-LRN-008--010).

Let $\operatorname{Valid}_{\ell,l}(X)$ mean that all input required to
interpret the strokes is available, and let
$\operatorname{Complete}_{\ell,l}(X)$ mean that every required part of
the drawing is present according to the lesson's fixed stage criteria.
These functions use the fixed completeness criteria of the selected lesson
and stage. Stage-result values use the normalized 0--100 scale specified in
Table~\ref{tab:goalconst}; the actual scoring function is defined for
each lesson's drawing criteria rather than as an assessment of a learner.
Define
\[
C_{\ell,l}(X)=
\begin{cases}
\textsf{invalid} & \neg\operatorname{Valid}_{\ell,l}(X),\\
\textsf{complete} & \operatorname{Valid}_{\ell,l}(X)\land
                      \operatorname{Complete}_{\ell,l}(X),\\
\textsf{incomplete} & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The guidance classification uses the level of the actual attempt:
\[
G(l)=\begin{cases}
\textsf{assisted} & l\in\{T,E\},\\
\textsf{independent} & l=I.
\end{cases}
\]
Let $v_{\ell,l}(X)\in[0,100]$ be the normalized stage result of an
evaluable drawing. Its threshold is
$\theta_p=\texttt{STAGE\_\allowbreak PASS}(p)$, taken from the adult's approved
profile value (or midpoint default under BR-008). The pass predicate is
\[
\operatorname{Pass}(X,l,p)\iff
C_{\ell,l}(X)=\textsf{complete}
\land v_{\ell,l}(X)\ge\theta_p.
\]
A missing, malformed or interrupted attempt never satisfies this
predicate. Non-passing but evaluable attempts contribute to the
unsuccessful-attempt count. Invalid or interrupted input is never interpreted as success and does
not contribute to a stage's evaluated-attempt or unsuccessful-attempt
counters. The presented feedback is selected from the fixed set of
observations in BR-007 rather than generated advice.

\paragraph{Improvement and suggestion predicates.}
Use the constants defined in Section~\ref{sec:constants}:
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_p &= \texttt{DIFFICULTY\_\allowbreak STREAK}(p), &
 b_p &= \texttt{ATTEMPT\_\allowbreak LIMIT}(p),\\
w_p &= \texttt{IMPROVE\_\allowbreak WINDOW}(p), &
 e_p &= \texttt{ATTEMPT\_\allowbreak EXTENSION}(p),\\
 h_p &= \texttt{ATTEMPT\_\allowbreak LIMIT\_\allowbreak MAX}(p), &
 \delta &= \texttt{IMPROVE\_\allowbreak MARGIN}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For $H=(r_1,\ldots,r_m)$, define the total improvement predicate
\[
\operatorname{Improving}(H,p)=
\begin{cases}
\textsf{false}, & m<2w_p,\\
\displaystyle
\left(\frac{1}{w_p}\sum_{i=m-w_p+1}^{m} r_i
-\frac{1}{w_p}\sum_{i=m-2w_p+1}^{m-w_p}r_i\ge\delta\right),
& m\ge 2w_p.
\end{cases}
\]
Insufficient history therefore does not count as improvement and does
not delay a difficulty suggestion. For the default $w_p=2$, the first
comparison uses four evaluable attempts; a shorter history still allows
the suggestion at the selected failure-streak threshold.

For an eligible return to a preceding guidance level, the suggestion
trigger is
\[
\operatorname{SuggestBack}(\sigma)\iff
q=\textsf{active}\land s=0\land l\in\{E,I\}\land k\ge d_p
\land\neg\operatorname{Improving}(H,p).
\]
The rule applies to drawing levels $E$ and $I$; $T$ has no preceding
drawing level. A suggestion changes $q$ to $\textsf{suggested}$ and $s$
to $1$, but \emph{does not change $l$}. Declining or ignoring a suggestion
resets the streak counter $k$ to zero, preventing an immediate re-offer;
a new suggestion requires another $d_p$ consecutive failures.

\paragraph{State-transition rules.}
Table~\ref{tab:lrn-transitions} gives the level-changing and
stage-stopping rules. Here $\sigma'$ is the state after the event,
``unchanged'' means the active lesson and level remain exactly as before,
and $\operatorname{AdultAccept}$ is a verified adult action rather than any
child drawing input. Likewise, $\operatorname{AdultSelect}$ is the
authorized action selecting a lesson and its starting level.
A granted extension sets $z=1$ once, with $x=0$; each subsequently
finalized evaluable attempt increments $x$. The extension cannot be
granted again at this stage even if later scores qualify as improvement.
After finalization, a passing result takes priority over stop and
suggestion rules. Otherwise, reaching the base failure limit first
triggers either the single extension grant (if improving and still below
the hard cap) or lesson end. After a grant, exhausting $x=e_p$ or
reaching $n_a=h_p$ ends the lesson. A suggestion is offered only when
none of these higher-priority rules ends the lesson.
For pass-based forward transitions define
$\operatorname{Next}(T)=E$ and $\operatorname{Next}(E)=I$; $I$ has no
successor within its lesson. Passing the last stage ends \emph{that} lesson.

\paragraph{Invariants and verification obligations.}
For every reachable session state and every adult- or learner-generated
event, the model requires:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\textbf{I1:}\quad \ell'\ne\ell
  \Longrightarrow\operatorname{AdultSelect};\\
&\textbf{I2:}\quad \text{suggestion ignored or declined}
  \Longrightarrow (\ell',l')=(\ell,l);\\
&\textbf{I3:}\quad C_{\ell,l}(X)\ne\textsf{complete}
  \Longrightarrow \neg\operatorname{Pass}(X,l,p);\\
&\textbf{I4:}\quad \text{pass/feedback never substitutes refined strokes
   for actual guided strokes};\\
&\textbf{I5:}\quad n_a\le h_p\ \land\ 0\le x\le e_p
   \land (z=0\Rightarrow x=0);\\
&\textbf{I6:}\quad \operatorname{Pass}(X,T,p)
  \Longrightarrow (\ell',l')=(\ell,E);\\
&\textbf{I7:}\quad \operatorname{Pass}(X,E,p)
  \Longrightarrow (\ell',l')=(\ell,I);\\
&\textbf{I8:}\quad \operatorname{Pass}(X,I,p)
  \Longrightarrow q'=\textsf{ended}\land\ell'=\ell.
\end{aligned}
\]
Outside ordinary pass-based progression and the demonstration-to-practice
transition, a change to the active stage requires an authorized adult
selection or acceptance (CON-007). Invariants I1 and I6--I8 ensure that
a new lesson is never selected automatically and that passing an attempt
advances only within the current lesson. I2--I5 cover suggestions, invalid
input, faithful records, bounded stage attempts and the one-time extension.
These rules are verified using prepared adult-made attempt sequences
(CON-005--006), including threshold cases with insufficient improvement
history, declined suggestions, a full extension and the hard cap in
Table~\ref{tab:goalconst}.

\subsection{Creative Drawing Requirements}
\label{sec:fr-creative}

This subsection gives the requirements for the creative space, where a
child draws freely beside the structured practice of the guided lessons.
Creative drawing gives room for self-expression, and keeping the drawing
editable keeps the child in control of the work (G.4; SRC-001). The
requirements make G.4 and BR-005 precise: when the child finishes a
drawing, Squigglr refines it automatically and minimally by adjusting the
child's own strokes, the refined drawing stays editable, and the child, or
the adult if the child cannot, can restore the original or turn
refinement off. Nothing in this
subsection applies to guided lessons, whose records keep the child's
actual strokes (BR-004). The requirements serve two product use cases
(UCs): UC-006, in which the child creates and edits artwork, and UC-007,
in which Squigglr refines artwork and the original can be restored. The
team checks every fit criterion on prepared, adult-made drawings, never
with children (CON-005, ASM-014).

The following terms have a fixed meaning in this subsection. A
\emph{creative drawing} is one drawing in the creative space, made of
strokes; each stroke has a path, a colour and a width. A \emph{part} is
one or more strokes that the child selects. A \emph{change} is one stroke
drawn, one editing action, one applied refinement or one restore. The
child \emph{finishes} a drawing by marking it as done; there is no
separate control for asking for refinement. A \emph{refinement} is one
automatic adjustment of the drawing's strokes, and its result is the
\emph{refined drawing}. The \emph{original} of a refinement is the
drawing exactly as it was immediately before that refinement was applied:
the same strokes in the same order, with the same points, colours and
widths. The \emph{supported set} is the agreed set of shapes and simple
objects that Squigglr refines (G.4); its contents are open issue OI-03
(Table~\ref{tab:openIssues}). \texttt{REFINE\_TOLERANCE} is defined in
Table~\ref{tab:goalconst}.

The first group covers what the child can do to a creative drawing,
whether or not it has been refined. These are the editing actions that
the Problem Statement lists as inputs (SRC-001) and that G.4
measurement (b) requires for refined parts.

\begin{srsrequirement}{FR-CRT-001}{Draw strokes}
  \reqshall{Squigglr shall add each stroke the child draws with the stylus in the
creative space to the open creative drawing as a new stroke.}
  \reqrationale{Drawing strokes is the basic input of the creative
space (SRC-001), and the stylus is the main input (CON-003).}
  \reqfit{After an adult tester draws a prepared sequence of
strokes, the drawing holds one new stroke for each stroke drawn, in the
order drawn; each follows the points the browser reported for it
(ASM-004) and can be selected
(\reqref{FR-CRT-002}).}
  \reqpriority{Must}
  \reqphase{PH-1}
  \reqsources{G.4; CON-003; \srcref{001}}
  \reqtraces{\ucref{006}; \reqref{FR-CRT-002}}
\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{FR-CRT-002}{Select a part}
  \reqshall{Squigglr shall let the child select one or more strokes of the open
creative drawing as the part to edit.}
  \reqrationale{Moving, resizing, recolouring and erasing act on a
chosen part of the drawing; the Problem Statement calls them editing
actions on drawing elements (SRC-001). No source names selection, but
every editing action needs it.}
  \reqfit{On a prepared drawing of at least two strokes, the
tester selects one stroke and then a group of strokes; each time, the
selected strokes are visibly marked, and the next editing action changes

  \reqpriority{Must}

The second group makes ``automatic'' and ``minimal'' in BR-005 testable,
and lets refinement be turned off (UC-007, UC-010).
Unless a fit criterion says otherwise, it is checked on prepared,

\begin{srsrequirement}{FR-CRT-010}{Refine a finished drawing}
  \reqshall{Squigglr shall refine the open creative drawing automatically each time
the child finishes it while refinement is turned on, and at no other time.}
  \reqrationale{Refinement is automatic and needs no action from the
child (BR-005), and UC-007 starts when the child finishes a drawing.
Refining only then means that strokes never move while the child is
still drawing, which could confuse a child or take the drawing out of
their hands. Both questionnaire respondents suggested that refinement
be optional or adjustable (SRC-009.01), so \reqref{FR-CRT-030} lets it be
turned off.}
  \reqfit{With refinement turned on, finishing each prepared drawing
starts exactly one refinement; drawing, editing, undoing, restoring and leaving the creative
space without finishing start none.}
  \reqpriority{Must}
  \reqphase{PH-1}

\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{FR-CRT-011}{Change only stroke paths}
  \reqshall{Squigglr shall change only the path of each existing stroke when it
refines a creative drawing.}
  \reqrationale{Refinement must be minimal and the work must remain the
child's own (BR-005). The team presented refinement to the specialists as
tidying the child's lines and curves, not swapping in a different picture
(SRC-005, \intref{Moustafa}{6:03}); the interview specialists did not
comment on it (FACT-013), but the questionnaire respondents asked that
refinement stay subtle and support the child's own drawing rather than
replace or correct it (SRC-009.01). Adding, removing or replacing strokes,
or changing their colours or widths, would make the drawing less the
child's own.}
  \reqfit{For each prepared drawing, the refined drawing has
the same number of strokes as its original, in the same order, and each
refined stroke has the colour and width of the corresponding original
stroke.}
  \reqpriority{Must}
  \reqphase{PH-1}
  \reqsources{G.4; BR-005; \srcref{005}, \intref{Moustafa}{6:03} (as

  \reqtraces{\ucref{007}; \reqref{FR-CRT-012}, \reqref{FR-CRT-020}; H-MUH-002}
\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{FR-CRT-014}{Do not refine a stroke twice}

\begin{srsrequirement}{FR-CRT-017}{Restore the exact original}
  \reqshall{Squigglr shall return a creative drawing, on restore, exactly to the

  \reqrationale{This makes G.4 measurement (c) precise. After one
refinement, a restore returns the child's drawing as it was before
refinement. After several, each restore steps back one refinement, so no
earlier version of the child's work is lost. A restore also removes edits
made after that refinement; because a restore is one change, the child
can undo it (\reqref{FR-CRT-007}) to get the refined and
edited drawing back.}
  \reqfit{For each prepared drawing: (a) after one refinement
and a restore, the drawing equals its original stroke for stroke and
point for point, in the same colours and widths; (b) after a refinement,
an edit, a restore and an undo, the drawing equals the refined and edited
drawing exactly; (c) after a refinement, new strokes, a second
refinement and two restores, the drawing equals the original of the
second refinement after the first restore and the original of the first
refinement after the second; (d) no restore is offered when no
refinement remains that has not been undone or restored.}
  \reqpriority{Must}
  \reqphase{PH-1}
  \reqsources{G.4; BR-005; \srcref{001}}

\end{srsrequirement}

  \reqsources{G.4; BR-005; \srcref{001}; \srcref{005}, \intref{Moustafa}{6:03}

The fourth group covers failure and interruption. A refinement may fail,
return an unusable result, or finish after the child has moved on, for
example when the connection is slow or lost (ASM-005). How quickly
refinement must respond, and how Squigglr copes when refinement is
unavailable, belong to the performance requirements
(Section~\ref{sec:performance}).

\begin{srsrequirement}{FR-CRT-020}{Leave the drawing unchanged when refinement fails}
  \reqshall{Squigglr shall make no change to a creative drawing when its refinement
fails or the refinement result violates
\reqref{FR-CRT-011} or \reqref{FR-CRT-012}.}
  \reqrationale{This restates G.4 measurement (d) for automatic
refinement, which the child never requests (BR-005). A partly applied or
invalid result could change the drawing more than minimally, so it is
applied whole or not at all. The Development Plan also requires that an
invalid result leave the drawing unchanged (SRC-002). The drawing stays
editable, and finishing it again starts a new refinement
(\reqref{FR-CRT-010}).}
  \reqfit{For each prepared drawing and each of these
simulated cases (lost connection; an error during refinement; no result
within the time limit of the speed requirements; a result that adds a
stroke; a result with a point outside \texttt{REFINE\_\allowbreak TOLERANCE}; a
malformed result), the drawing afterwards equals the drawing as finished,
can be edited (\reqref{FR-CRT-002} to
\reqref{FR-CRT-007}) and, from PH-2, when
\reqref{FR-CRT-016} applies, is shown as not refined.}
  \reqpriority{Must}
  \reqphase{PH-1}
  \reqsources{G.4; BR-005; ASM-005; \srcref{002}}
  \reqtraces{\ucref{007}; \reqref{FR-CRT-011}, \reqref{FR-CRT-012}; H-MUH-002}
\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{FR-CRT-021}{Discard an out-of-date result}
  \reqshall{Squigglr shall discard a refinement result if the drawing has been
changed or closed since that refinement started.}
  \reqrationale{A result computed from an older version of the drawing
would remove the child's newer strokes, or change a drawing the child
has left. Discarding it keeps the drawing as the child made it; the child
can finish again. No source states this case.}
  \reqfit{For each prepared drawing, with the result delayed
by the test set-up: (a) if the tester draws a stroke before the result
arrives, the drawing afterwards is the finished drawing plus that stroke,
unrefined; (b) if the tester leaves the creative space before the result
arrives, reopening the drawing shows it exactly as left, unrefined.}
  \reqpriority{Must}
  \reqphase{PH-2}
  \reqsources{G.4; BR-005; ASM-005}
  \reqtraces{\ucref{006}, \ucref{007}; \reqref{FR-CRT-022}, \reqref{FR-CRT-024}; H-MUH-002}
\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{FR-CRT-022}{Keep drawing while refinement runs}
  \reqshall{Squigglr shall let the child keep drawing and editing while a refinement
is in progress.}
  \reqrationale{A child kept waiting may lose focus (PER-1), and
children should be able to use Squigglr without constant adult help
(SRC-005, \intref{P1}{34:13}). No source states this case.}
  \reqfit{With the result delayed by the test set-up, the
tester draws a stroke and moves a part before the result arrives; both
changes meet \reqref{FR-CRT-001} and
\reqref{FR-CRT-003}, and
\reqref{FR-CRT-021} then applies.}
  \reqpriority{Should}
  \reqphase{PH-2}
  \reqsources{G.4; \srcref{005}, \intref{P1}{34:13}}
  \reqtraces{\ucref{006}, \ucref{007}; \reqref{FR-CRT-021}}
\end{srsrequirement}

The fifth group covers saving and reopening. UC-006 ends with the drawing
saved and still editable. One account can hold several learner profiles
and a device may be shared by several children (BR-001; SRC-004), so each
creative drawing belongs to one learner profile.

\begin{srsrequirement}{FR-CRT-023}{Save to the active learner}
  \reqshall{Squigglr shall save each creative drawing, with the originals of its
refinements, to the learner profile that was active when the child drew
it, without any action from the child.}
  \reqrationale{A child who cannot read may leave without saving.
Restoring after reopening needs the originals
(\reqref{FR-CRT-024}). Saving to the wrong learner would
show one child's work as another's (BR-001).}
  \reqfit{For two learner profiles in one prepared account,
the tester draws, refines and edits a drawing under each and leaves the
creative space with its own controls; reopening each learner's creative
space shows that learner's drawing exactly as left, and not the other
learner's.}
  \reqpriority{Must}
  \reqphase{PH-2}
  \reqsources{G.4; BR-001; BR-005}

\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{FR-CRT-024}{Reopen a drawing as it was left}
  \reqshall{Squigglr shall reopen a saved creative drawing exactly as it was last
saved, with every stroke editable and every refinement that has not been
undone or restored still restorable.}
  \reqrationale{UC-006 requires the drawing to stay editable, and
BR-005 requires the original to stay restorable; neither ends when the
child leaves the creative space.}
  \reqfit{For prepared drawings saved in each state listed in
\reqref{FR-CRT-016}, reopening gives the same strokes and

  \reqsources{G.4; BR-005}

\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{FR-CRT-025}{Keep changes through a lost connection}
  \reqshall{Squigglr shall save, once the connection returns, every change the child

  \reqfit{With a prepared drawing open, the test set-up cuts

  \reqpriority{Should}
  \reqphase{PH-3}

  \reqtraces{\ucref{006}; \reqref{FR-CRT-023}; H-MUH-005}
\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{FR-CRT-029}{Show unsaved changes}
  \reqshall{Squigglr shall show, while a creative drawing is open, whether every change

  \reqrationale{Offline use is out of scope (ASM-005), so changes made

\section{Look and Feel Requirements}
\label{sec:look-feel}
% Long constant names cannot be hyphenated; let lines stretch rather than
% overflow the margin in this section.
\begingroup
\sloppy

This section states how Squigglr looks (Section~\ref{sec:appearance}) and
the mood its style creates (Section~\ref{sec:style}). A
\emph{child-facing screen} is any screen the child uses in a guided lesson
or the creative space; an \emph{adult view} is any screen for setup,
settings, records or progress summaries. No requirement is gathered from
children and no study with children takes place (CON-005, CON-006), so
the fit criteria are checked by inspecting every screen or by adult
reviewers. An adult review shows that adults find a design suitable, not
that children can use it; checking that needs a study with ethics
approval, which is out of scope (\srcref{005.11}).

The specialists advised starting from the Web Content Accessibility
Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2~\cite{wcag22} (\srcref{005.08}), and the supervisor
advised taking specific WCAG ideas as non-functional requirements rather
than treating WCAG as a constraint (\srcref{008}). Each requirement below
that is based on a WCAG success criterion names it in its rationale.
Further WCAG-based accessibility criteria are in
Section~\ref{sec:usability}, and listing WCAG as a standard is open issue
OI-08.

\subsection{Appearance Requirements}
\label{sec:appearance}

\begin{srsrequirement}{NFR-UX-001}{Calm, uncluttered child screens}
  \reqshall{Each child-facing screen shall offer the child no more than
\texttt{CHILD\_CONTROLS\_MAX} controls and shall show no decorative
animation or flashing while the child is drawing.}
  \reqrationale{Too many visual or auditory stimuli can overwhelm children,
and specialists advised a calm, minimally stimulating interface
(\srcref{009.05}). A child who cannot read navigates by images, so every
extra control is something more to interpret (\srcref{005.05}). Banning
flashing and moving content while the child draws follows WCAG 2.2
success criteria 2.3.1 Three Flashes or Below Threshold and 2.2.2 Pause,
Stop, Hide~\cite{wcag22}.}
  \reqfit{Inspect every child-facing screen in each lesson stage and in the
creative space. Pass when each screen offers at most
\texttt{CHILD\_CONTROLS\_MAX} controls and no element animates or
flashes while a stroke is being drawn.}
  \reqpriority{Must}
  \reqphase{PH-2}
  \reqsources{\goalref{1}; \srcref{005.05}; \srcref{009.05}}
  \reqtraces{\ucref{003}; \ucref{006}; NFR-UX-002; SAF-MOU-003;
H-MOU-001}
\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{NFR-UX-002}{Images and icons instead of text}
  \reqshall{Every control on a child-facing screen shall be identified by
an image or icon, and no task on a child-facing screen shall require the
child to read.}
  \reqrationale{Many learners cannot read yet; children navigate games
through images and icons rather than text (\srcref{005.05}, FACT-007).}
  \reqfit{Inspect every child-facing screen. Pass when every control shows
an image or icon, and each child task in \ucref{003} and \ucref{006} can be
completed with all on-screen text hidden.}
  \reqpriority{Must}
  \reqphase{PH-2}
  \reqsources{\goalref{1}; FACT-007; \srcref{005.05}}
  \reqtraces{\ucref{003}; \ucref{006}; NFR-UX-001; H-MOU-003}
\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{NFR-UX-003}{Adult views distinct from child screens}
  \reqshall{Adult views shall look clearly different from child-facing
screens, so that an adult can tell at a glance which kind of screen is
shown.}
  \reqrationale{Specialists described a separate adult interface for
personalizing activities and viewing progress (\srcref{005.02}). A
distinct look helps adults notice when a child has reached an adult-only
screen, alongside the adult check (SAF-RAY-001).}
  \reqfit{Show at least \texttt{UX\_REVIEWERS} adult reviewers a shuffled
set of screenshots of every child-facing screen and adult view, without
titles. Pass when every reviewer sorts every screenshot correctly.}
  \reqpriority{Should}
  \reqphase{PH-2}
  \reqsources{\goalref{3}, \goalref{6}; \srcref{005.02}}
  \reqtraces{\ucref{001}; \ucref{002}; \ucref{009}; \ucref{010};
SAF-RAY-001}
\end{srsrequirement}

\subsection{Style Requirements}
\label{sec:style}

\begin{srsrequirement}{NFR-UX-004}{Feedback that never marks the drawing
as wrong}
  \reqshall{Feedback on an attempt, including a missed part or a
non-passing attempt, shall use encouraging styling, shall not use error
styling such as warning colours, crosses, alarm sounds or ``wrong''
symbols, and shall not show a missed part by colour alone.}
  \reqrationale{Children become frustrated when an activity keeps
highlighting their mistakes or suggests that their drawing is wrong, so
feedback should recognize effort instead (\srcref{009.02}). Feedback
wording and colours should be positive (\srcref{005.06}). Feedback
describes the drawing, not the child
(BR-006, \mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-022}}). Showing a missed part by more than
colour follows WCAG 2.2 success criterion 1.4.1 Use of
Color~\cite{wcag22}, so a child who does not see the colour still sees
the missed part.}
  \reqfit{For each predefined difficulty, and for a passing and a
non-passing attempt, show a prepared drawing and inspect the feedback.
Pass when every feedback includes an encouraging cue from the approved
feedback list (\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-014}}), none uses an element on the
error-styling list in this requirement, and every missed part is marked by
an outline, pattern or icon as well as by colour.}
  \reqpriority{Must}
  \reqphase{PH-1}
  \reqsources{\goalref{1}; \srcref{005.06}; \srcref{009.02}}
  \reqtraces{\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-014}}; \mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-022}};
\ucref{003}; H-AHM-005; H-MOU-002; H-MOU-003}
\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{NFR-UX-005}{Consistent look across child screens}
  \reqshall{The same action shall use the same icon, colour and screen
position on every child-facing screen.}
  \reqrationale{Children stay motivated when activities are predictable
(\srcref{009.02}), and a child who navigates by images relies on each icon
meaning the same thing everywhere (\srcref{005.05}). This follows WCAG 2.2
success criteria 3.2.3 Consistent Navigation and 3.2.4 Consistent
Identification~\cite{wcag22}.}
  \reqfit{List every action available on child-facing screens and inspect
each screen where it appears. Pass when each action has one icon, one
colour and one position across all screens.}
  \reqpriority{Should}
  \reqphase{PH-2}
  \reqsources{\srcref{005.05}; \srcref{009.02}}
  \reqtraces{\ucref{003}; \ucref{006}; NFR-UX-002}
\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{NFR-UX-006}{Appealing but calm style}
  \reqshall{Child-facing screens shall have a style that adult reviewers
judge both appealing to children and calm.}
  \reqrationale{Specialists warned that the design may not be appealing
enough for children, and that the right balance is a playful design in a
calm, minimally stimulating environment (\srcref{009.05}).}
  \reqfit{Show at least \texttt{UX\_REVIEWERS} adult reviewers every
child-facing screen and ask them to rate how appealing to children and
how calm each is, on a five-point scale. Pass when the median of each
rating is at least \texttt{STYLE\_RATING\_MIN}.}
  \reqpriority{Should}
  \reqphase{PH-3}
  \reqsources{\srcref{009.05}}
  \reqtraces{NFR-UX-001; NFR-UX-007; H-MOU-001}
\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{NFR-UX-007}{Style reviewed with educators or
therapists}
  \reqshall{The colours, images and feedback style of child-facing screens
shall be reviewed by at least \texttt{UX\_REVIEWERS} adults, including at
least one educator or occupational therapist, and each change they
request shall be made or recorded with a reason.}
  \reqrationale{Specialists advised designing feedback wording and colours
with educators and occupational therapists, built up bit by bit
(\srcref{005.06}), and involving them throughout design (\srcref{009.06}).
None has been consulted yet (FACT-012).}
  \reqfit{Pass when the review record lists the reviewers and their roles,
includes at least one educator or occupational therapist, and states for
every requested change either that it was made or the reason it was
not.}
  \reqpriority{Should}
  \reqphase{PH-3}
  \reqsources{\srcref{005.06}; \srcref{009.06}; FACT-012}
  \reqtraces{NFR-UX-004; NFR-UX-006; H-MOU-001; H-MOU-002}
\end{srsrequirement}

This section states how easy Squigglr is to use, personalize and learn,
how its wording treats children and adults, and which accessibility
criteria it meets. As in Section~\ref{sec:look-feel}, fit criteria are

\subsection{Ease of Use Requirements}
\label{sec:ease-of-use}

\begin{srsrequirement}{NFR-UX-008}{Practice without adult help}
  \reqshall{Once an adult has started a lesson, Squigglr shall need no
adult action through every stage and retry until the lesson ends, other
than a response to a suggestion to go back a stage.}
  \reqrationale{Children should be able to practise without constant
adult help, and parents quickly become annoyed if they must keep

\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-023}}).}

\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{NFR-UX-009}{Quick setup by a new adult}

\texttt{SETUP\_TIME\_MAX} minutes, with no help from outside Squigglr.}
  \reqrationale{Adults handle setup (\srcref{005.05}), and they are

practice.}

at least \texttt{UX\_PASS\_RATE} of them finish within

  \reqpriority{Should}
  \reqphase{PH-2}
  \reqsources{\goalref{3}; \srcref{005.05}}
  \reqtraces{\ucref{001}; \ucref{002}; NFR-UX-014}
\end{srsrequirement}

\subsection{Understandability and Politeness Requirements}
\label{sec:politeness}

\begin{srsrequirement}{NFR-UX-015}{Short, encouraging messages to the
child}
  \reqshall{Every message to the child, including feedback on an attempt
and the end of a lesson, shall be encouraging, shall describe the drawing
rather than the child, and shall be at most \texttt{FEEDBACK\_WORDS\_MAX}
words when spoken.}
  \reqrationale{Feedback wording should be positive (\srcref{005.06}), and
children become frustrated when their drawing is called wrong
(\srcref{009.02}). Feedback describes the drawing, not the child (BR-006,
\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-022}}). A lesson that ends at its limit must not feel
like a failure (SAF-RAY-005). Messages are short because they are heard
by children who may not read.}
  \reqfit{List every approved feedback message
(\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-014}}) and the lesson-end message. Pass when each
has at most \texttt{FEEDBACK\_WORDS\_MAX} words, refers to the drawing and
not the child, and uses none of the words ``wrong'', ``bad'', ``fail'' or
``mistake''.}
  \reqpriority{Must}
  \reqphase{PH-1}
  \reqsources{\goalref{1}; BR-006; \srcref{005.06}; \srcref{009.02}}
  \reqtraces{\mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-014}}; \mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-022}};
NFR-UX-004; \ucref{003}; H-AHM-005; H-MOU-002; H-MOU-003}
\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{NFR-UX-016}{Effort acknowledged}
  \reqshall{The feedback on every evaluable attempt, including one that
does not pass, shall acknowledge the child's effort before it shows what
to change.}
  \reqrationale{Squigglr should recognize effort and progress, not only
accuracy (\srcref{009.02}).}
  \reqfit{For each predefined difficulty, show a prepared non-passing
attempt. Pass when every feedback message begins with an acknowledgement
from the approved feedback list.}
  \reqpriority{Should}
  \reqphase{PH-2}
  \reqsources{\srcref{009.02}}
  \reqtraces{NFR-UX-015; \mbox{\reqref{FR-LRN-014}}; \ucref{003};
H-MOU-002}
\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{NFR-UX-017}{Plain wording in adult views}
  \reqshall{Adult views shall use plain language, shall explain each
glossary term where it first appears in a view, and shall not use
diagnostic, clinical or ranking words.}
  \reqrationale{The TA asked that terms such as ``guidance level'' be
defined rather than left as jargon (\srcref{006}). Records describe
practice only and are not an assessment (BR-006, SAF-RAY-004), and adults
are not assumed to have clinical training
(Section~\ref{sec:stakeholders}).}
  \reqfit{Inspect every adult view against the glossary
(Section~\ref{sec:glossary}) and the words ``diagnosis'', ``disorder'',
``delay'', ``score'', ``rank'' and ``below average''. Pass when every
glossary term used is explained in the view and none of the listed words
appears.}
  \reqpriority{Must}
  \reqphase{PH-2}
  \reqsources{BR-006; \srcref{006}}
  \reqtraces{SAF-RAY-004; \ucref{009}; \ucref{010}; H-MOU-005}
\end{srsrequirement}

\subsection{Accessibility Requirements}
\label{sec:accessibility}

These requirements take specific WCAG 2.2 success
criteria~\cite{wcag22} that the team can check without children, as the
specialists and the supervisor advised (\srcref{005.08}, \srcref{008});
full WCAG conformance is not required. Together with NFR-UX-001,
NFR-UX-004 and NFR-UX-005, they are the criteria Squigglr follows
(OI-08). The stylus is the main input (CON-003); finger input is a
stretch goal and is not required here.

\begin{srsrequirement}{NFR-UX-018}{Large touch targets}
  \reqshall{Every control on a child-facing screen shall have a touch
target of at least \texttt{TARGET\_SIZE\_MIN}.}
  \reqrationale{Children's grip and fine motor control vary
(\srcref{005.04}), and the supervisor named large icons as a WCAG idea to
adopt (\srcref{008}). The size follows WCAG 2.2 success criterion 2.5.5
Target Size (Enhanced)~\cite{wcag22}.}
  \reqfit{Measure every control on every child-facing screen at the
smallest supported screen size. Pass when every touch target is at least
\texttt{TARGET\_SIZE\_MIN}.}
  \reqpriority{Must}
  \reqphase{PH-2}
  \reqsources{\srcref{005.04}; \srcref{008}}
  \reqtraces{NFR-UX-002; \ucref{003}; \ucref{006}}
\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{NFR-UX-019}{Colour contrast}
  \reqshall{On every screen, text shall have a contrast ratio of at least
4.5:1 with its background, and icons and graphics needed to use a control
at least 3:1.}
  \reqrationale{Some children have sight challenges (\srcref{005.09}). The
ratios follow WCAG 2.2 success criteria 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum) and
1.4.11 Non-text Contrast~\cite{wcag22}.}
  \reqfit{Check every screen with a contrast checker. Pass when every
piece of text and every control graphic meets its ratio.}
  \reqpriority{Must}
  \reqphase{PH-2}
  \reqsources{\srcref{005.08}; \srcref{005.09}}
  \reqtraces{NFR-UX-001; NFR-UX-004}
\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{NFR-UX-020}{Text alternatives for icons}
  \reqshall{Every icon or image that acts as a control shall have a text
alternative that assistive technology can read.}
  \reqrationale{An adult who uses a screen reader must still be able to set
up and review practice, and designing for disability serves many groups
(\srcref{005.09}). This follows WCAG 2.2 success criterion 1.1.1 Non-text
Content~\cite{wcag22}. The child does not need the text
(NFR-UX-002).}
  \reqfit{Run an automated accessibility checker on every screen and
inspect each control. Pass when every control has a text alternative that
names its action.}
  \reqpriority{Should}
  \reqphase{PH-2}
  \reqsources{\srcref{005.08}; \srcref{005.09}}
  \reqtraces{NFR-UX-002; NFR-UX-012}
\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{NFR-UX-021}{Feedback not by sound alone}
  \reqshall{Every message to the child shall be shown on screen and, unless
audio is turned off, also played as sound; no message shall rely on sound
alone.}
  \reqrationale{Offering more than one modality is encouraged in
inclusive design, and the user can turn the audio off (\srcref{005.07}),
so turning it off must not hide any feedback (NFR-UX-010). This follows
WCAG 2.2 success criterion 1.3.3 Sensory Characteristics~\cite{wcag22}.}
  \reqfit{With audio on and then off, show every approved feedback message
and the lesson-end message. Pass when each appears on screen both times,
and sound plays only when audio is on.}
  \reqpriority{Must}
  \reqphase{PH-2}
  \reqsources{\srcref{005.07}}
  \reqtraces{NFR-UX-010; NFR-UX-015; \ucref{003}; H-MOU-003}
\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{NFR-UX-022}{Control of audio}
  \reqshall{Any sound that plays automatically for more than 3 seconds

  \reqrationale{Too much auditory stimulation can overwhelm children
(\srcref{009.05}). This follows WCAG 2.2 success criterion 1.4.2 Audio

  \reqfit{List every sound Squigglr plays. Pass when each sound longer than

  \reqpriority{Should}
  \reqphase{PH-2}
  \reqsources{\srcref{005.08}; \srcref{009.05}}
  \reqtraces{NFR-UX-010; H-MOU-001}
\end{srsrequirement}

\begin{srsrequirement}{NFR-UX-023}{Keyboard use of adult views}

  \reqtraces{NFR-UX-014}
\end{srsrequirement}

\subsection{Safety-Critical Requirements}
This subsection holds safety requirements that come from the Hazard Analysis
and do not fit another section. Each lists the hazards it mitigates.

\begin{description}
\item[SAF-RAY-001] Squigglr shall allow lesson choice, starting-stage
choice, learner switching and acceptance of a go-back suggestion only after
an adult check that a child cannot complete by tapping icons, and shall keep
a pending suggestion, without blocking practice, until an adult accepts or
declines it.\\
\emph{Rationale:} children should be able to practise without constant
adult help (SRC-005, \intref{P1}{34:13}), but progression belongs to adults
(CON-007; SRC-005, \intref{P1}{28:37}). The form of the check is
\texttt{ADULT\_CHECK}, TBD.\\
\emph{Fit criterion:} in prepared walkthroughs, no adult-only action
completes without passing the adult check, and a pending suggestion changes
nothing until an adult acts.\\
\emph{Hazards:} H-RAY-001, H-RAY-006.
\item[SAF-RAY-002] Before a lesson starts, Squigglr shall check the device
and browser against \texttt{SUPPORTED\_DEVICES} (TBD) and, if they do not
match, show an adult-readable notice that results may be unreliable.\\
\emph{Rationale:} the team does not control the device (CON-001--CON-003).\\
\emph{Fit criterion:} on each prepared unsupported combination the notice
appears before the first attempt; on supported ones it does not.\\
\emph{Hazards:} H-RAY-002.
\item[SAF-RAY-003] Squigglr shall keep each completed attempt on the device
until the server confirms it is saved, and shall tell the adult which
attempts could not be saved.\\
\emph{Rationale:} offline access is not promised (SRC-005,
\intref{Moustafa}{6:03}), and G.5 needs complete records.\\
\emph{Fit criterion:} with the connection cut during or after prepared
attempts, no confirmed attempt is lost and every unsaved attempt is reported
to the adult.\\
\emph{Hazards:} H-RAY-002.
\item[SAF-RAY-004] No record, summary or export shall show a
diagnostic label, a judgement of the child's abilities or a comparison with
other children, and every summary and
export shall state that it describes practice only and is not an assessment
or diagnosis.\\
\emph{Rationale:} Squigglr makes no diagnosis (SRC-001; SRC-005,
\intref{Moustafa}{6:03}), and adults reading records may have no training.\\
\emph{Fit criterion:} inspection of every summary and export template finds
none of the excluded content and finds the statement present.\\
\emph{Hazards:} H-RAY-003.
\item[SAF-RAY-005] Squigglr shall end a lesson at \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT},
or at \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT\_MAX} when extended, without any action from
the child, and shall keep the progress saved up to that point.\\
\emph{Rationale:} the child is not expected to exit a lesson, and no adult
may be watching (CA-001), so repeated failure must end on its own (G.3).\\
\emph{Fit criterion:} in prepared sequences of failed attempts, each lesson
ends at the limit in use with no child input, and the saved record matches
the attempts made.\\
\emph{Hazards:} H-RAY-004.
\item[SAF-RAY-006] Squigglr shall record each lesson that ends at
\texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT} or \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT\_MAX}, and each
improvement extension, show the adult how many times these happened for
each lesson, and keep earlier attempts when a lesson is restarted.\\
\emph{Rationale:} a restart could otherwise hide repeated failure from the
adult.\\
\emph{Fit criterion:} in prepared sequences that reach the limit and then
restart, the count and all earlier attempts appear in the adult view.\\
\emph{Hazards:} H-RAY-005.
\item[SAF-RAY-007] Squigglr shall identify the active learner on every
practice screen in a form a child who cannot read can recognize, and shall
save every attempt and suggestion to that learner only.\\
\emph{Rationale:} school devices may be shared by several children
(SRC-004).\\
\emph{Fit criterion:} in prepared multi-learner sessions on one device,
every attempt and suggestion is saved under the learner shown when it was
made.\\
\emph{Hazards:} H-RAY-006.
\item[SAF-RAY-008] Squigglr shall save a child's adult-set values
(Table~\ref{tab:goalconst}) only within their team-set ranges, with
\texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT} larger than \texttt{DIFFICULTY\_STREAK} and
\texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT\_MAX} at least \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT}; shall use the middle of each range when the
adult does not choose; shall allow changes only after the adult check; and
shall show the values in use alongside the child's records.\\
\emph{Rationale:} the supervisor advised that the adult chooses these
values (SRC-008); a wrong value can stop a child passing a stage they can
draw, and a child must not change it.\\
\emph{Fit criterion:} in prepared walkthroughs, every out-of-range value and
every \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT} not larger than \texttt{DIFFICULTY\_STREAK}
and every \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT\_MAX} below \texttt{ATTEMPT\_LIMIT} is
refused, an unset value equals the midpoint, and no change completes
without the adult check.\\
\emph{Hazards:} H-RAY-007.

\item[SAF-AHM-001] Squigglr shall prevent duplicate reports of the same
stylus input event from being counted more than once during evaluation or
recording of an attempt, while preserving legitimate separately drawn
strokes, including overlapping strokes.\\
\emph{Rationale:} duplicate input events can inflate drawing coverage,
produce incorrect pass or failure decisions, and cause saved records to
differ from what the learner actually drew (H-AHM-002; BR-004).\\
\emph{Fit criterion:} replay a known one-stroke or multi-stroke input
sequence with and without duplicated input events; verify that the
recorded points, classification and pass result are identical. Replay
two separate deliberately overlapping strokes and verify that both
are preserved.\\
\emph{Related SRS requirements:} FR-LRN-007, FR-LRN-008.\\
\emph{Hazards:} H-AHM-002.

\item[SAF-AHM-002] Squigglr shall preserve the relative positions and order
of stylus points within the active drawing surface when the canvas size
or supported device orientation changes, and shall reject input whose
coordinates cannot be mapped reliably to that surface.\\
\emph{Rationale:} an incorrect coordinate mapping can distort a learner's
drawing, produce an inaccurate passing decision, and corrupt stored
attempts (H-AHM-003; BR-004).\\
\emph{Fit criterion:} replay the same prepared stroke sequences before and
after resizing the drawing surface or changing supported device
orientation; verify equivalent relative point positions, stroke order
and pass classifications. Supply invalid or unmappable coordinate values
and verify that none is classified as passing or counted as an evaluable
attempt.\\
\emph{Related SRS requirements:} FR-LRN-007, FR-LRN-010.\\
\emph{Hazards:} H-AHM-003.

\item[SAF-MOU-001] In guided lessons, feedback on a missed part shall show
the child where the part is missing without drawing it for them.\\
\emph{Rationale:} the aim of practice is drawing with gradually less help
(G.2); if Squigglr completes the drawing, the child can come to rely on it
(H-MOU-004). The child's actual strokes are what is recorded (BR-004).\\
\emph{Fit criterion:} for each predefined difficulty, show a prepared
attempt with a missed part and compare the strokes on screen and in the
record before and after the feedback. Pass when the feedback adds no
stroke to the attempt.\\
\emph{Related SRS requirements:} FR-LRN-014, FR-LRN-023, BR-004.\\
\emph{Hazards:} H-MOU-004.

\item[SAF-MOU-002] Records and progress summaries shall keep attempts
made with a guide separate from attempts made without one,
shall not include creative-space drawings, and shall not present the
number of attempts as a measure of the child's skill.\\
\emph{Rationale:} a summary that blurs assisted and independent attempts,
counts refined drawings as practice or treats fewer attempts as better
skill can lead an adult to move the child on too early or hold them back
(H-MOU-005; SRC-005, \intref{Moustafa}{6:03}). Refinement must never reach
the records adults rely on (BR-005).\\
\emph{Fit criterion:} for prepared histories with known assisted,
independent and creative-space work, check every summary and record. Pass
when assisted and independent attempts are shown separately, no
creative-space drawing appears, and no view ranks or rates the child by
the number of attempts.\\
\emph{Related SRS requirements:} G.6, FR-LRN-012, SAF-RAY-004,
NFR-UX-017.\\
\emph{Hazards:} H-MOU-005.

\item[SAF-MOU-003] No content on any child-facing screen shall flash more
than three times in any one-second period.\\
\emph{Rationale:} flashing can trigger seizures in rare cases, the only
physical harm in the hazard analysis (H-MOU-001). NFR-UX-001 bans flashing
while the child is drawing, but feedback, the lesson-end message and screen
transitions also appear on child-facing screens. This follows WCAG 2.2
success criterion 2.3.1 Three Flashes or Below Threshold~\cite{wcag22}.\\
\emph{Fit criterion:} record every child-facing screen, including all
feedback, the lesson-end message and every transition, and count the
flashes in each one-second window. Pass when no window has more than three
flashes.\\
\emph{Related SRS requirements:} NFR-UX-001.\\
\emph{Hazards:} H-MOU-001.

\end{description}

\item What pain points did you experience during this deliverable, and how did
you resolve them?

\textbf{Rayan:} The main pain point was that decisions kept changing as we
learned more, and each change affected several sections at once. In our
interview on October 1, 2026, the specialists said that the supporting adult,
not the app, should decide when a child moves to an easier or harder stage, so
we changed our progression goal from automatic to adult-controlled. In our
meeting on October 14, 2026, Dr.~Anand advised that thresholds such as the
pass criteria and the number of failed attempts should be set by the adult for
each child within a range the team defines, which changed several constraints,
business rules and goals. The answers to the follow-up questionnaire that
Moustafa and I sent the specialists, returned between October 2 and 4, 2026,
then confirmed that letters belong in a later stage after shapes, so we kept
them as a stretch goal and recorded the order of stages as a likely change. I resolved
this by updating every affected section in the same pull request as each
change, and by recording anything still unsettled in Open Issues (Section 18),
so that nothing was left half-changed. A smaller problem was that several of
my branches were open at once and each added a row to the revision history, so
later pull requests conflicted on that table; I merged main into each branch
and kept every row. I also found that some early drafts included details
nobody had actually discussed, so I went back through each section and removed
any claim I could not trace to a source or a team decision. Looking back, I
learned that requirements are never really finished early in a project, and
that keeping each change in one place and tracing it to its source is what
makes them manageable.

\textbf{Ahmed:} My biggest difficulty was keeping everything
